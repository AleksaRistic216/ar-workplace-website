# Reviewer outreach

Ten emails, written individually, sent once. This is not a campaign and there is no sequence.

## Rules

- **One email per person. No follow-up.** A second email to someone who did not reply is the line
  between outreach and spam, and this audience talks to each other.
- **Watch or read something of theirs first**, and say something specific about it that could not
  be copy-pasted into another email. If you cannot, they are not the right person.
- **Offer a licence, ask for nothing.** No "in exchange for a review", no embargo, no talking
  points. A reviewer who feels bought says so on camera.
- **Say it is closed source, paid, Linux and Windows only** in the email. Finding out later wastes
  their time and yours.
- **GDPR:** a business address published for contact is a legitimate-interest send. Keep it to one
  message, keep a note of where you found the address, and honour any request to stop instantly.
- Never buy a list. Never scrape addresses.

## Template

Rewrite every line of this. If the email could go to two people unchanged, it is not ready.

```
Subject: CPT — a terminal built around Windows/Linux parity

Hi <name>,

<One specific sentence about something they made. Not "love your content".>

I built Cross Platform Terminal, a terminal and dockable workspace with the same
shortcuts and layout on Windows and Linux, and per-pane status for agent CLIs —
it detects Claude Code, Copilot, Codex, Gemini, Aider and a few others behind
npx/uv/node shims and shows whether each pane is working, waiting on you, or failed.

Straight about the downsides: it's paid (€7.49/mo), closed source, no macOS build
yet, and payment is crypto-only.

If you'd like a licence to poke at, say the word and I'll send one, no strings and
no expectation of coverage. If it's not for you, no reply needed.

<name>
<link>
```

## What good looks like

One reply in ten is a good rate. One video is worth more than the whole social pipeline for a
quarter. If nobody replies, the problem is usually that the emails were too similar to each other —
that is a signal to write five better ones, not fifty more.
